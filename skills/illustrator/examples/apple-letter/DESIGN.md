## Overview

Apple Letter treats a simple apple mark as the sign on a personal note. The result is a small gesture: a gift, a memory, or a sentence waiting for one person. Keep the mood quiet, fresh, and slightly poetic through modern restraint, generous negative space, and tactile print composition rather than a complex illustrative metaphor.

## Colors

Let apple red provide the emotional center on a warm paper ground. Use leaf and tea greens as calm structural colors, with blush appearing only as a soft transition or secondary plane. Keep ink nearly black but slightly warm. Most compositions should use paper, ink, apple red, and only one green.

## Typography

Use Noto Sans SC with light weights and calm spacing. Build character through short lines, considered line breaks, vertical placement, and contrast between one large phrase and one intimate note. Use JetBrains Mono only for a date, variety name, coordinates, or a tiny edition mark. Do not simulate serif or handwritten lettering.

## Layout

Compose the image like a small art edition, contemporary book cover, or thoughtfully wrapped gift. Place one simple apple mark off center or crop it against the canvas edge, then balance it with a short message. Use one paper-colored field, one narrow color band, or one fine rule to create depth without turning the composition into a card layout.

## Mark and Material

Draw the apple as one confident silhouette with a small leaf and stem. Prefer a clean SVG path or equally simple geometry. Build tactile character around the mark with one or two print devices: a slightly offset color plate, sparse halftone dots, thin registration lines, or layered paper fields. Keep these effects visibly restrained; do not compensate for a weak silhouette by adding internal details, facial features, highlights, or literal letter and envelope symbols.

## Supplied Images

When an image is supplied, crop it as one quiet still-life plane or isolate a useful object against the paper ground. Echo one color from the image in the apple mark or text. Do not place a photo inside a generic card, collage it with multiple snapshots, or cover it with lifestyle-app interface elements.

## Components

Use one apple mark as the focal visual and one compact print label only when the composition benefits from an edition note or short descriptor. Treat the message itself as a component through placement and scale. Code images may use a pale paper canvas, a thin apple-red rule, and one small apple mark while leaving the syntax area neutral and highly legible.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do make the image feel addressed to one person, even when the copy is general.
- Do favor one poetic visual idea and one short message.
- Do use print texture and paper layers to enrich a simple mark.
- Don't copy an existing tea brand's logo, mascot, packaging, or campaign composition.
- Don't fill the canvas with fruit, leaves, labels, dots, and slogans at equal weight.
- Don't embed an envelope, letter, or other secondary icon inside the apple.
- Don't introduce outlined display type or a photo-frame system that competes with the quiet composition.
