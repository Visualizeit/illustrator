# Visual Direction

Treat each image as a small piece of art direction, not a decorated template.

## Read the Context

Infer the subject, audience, destination, and desired feeling from the available context. Draw visual cues from the subject's own language, materials, symbols, and environment. Respect supplied brand assets and natural-language direction.

## Commit to One Direction

Form a one-sentence visual premise before rendering. Choose one focal idea, a dominant composition, a palette strategy, a typographic voice, and a coherent material language. Let hierarchy, scale, rhythm, and negative space support that premise.

Use typography as part of the image rather than a neutral label. Make structural and decorative elements carry meaning. Prefer one memorable visual device over many unrelated effects, and match the level of detail to the intended viewing size.

## Resist Defaults

Do not reach automatically for purple-blue gradients, cream editorial layouts, black backgrounds with acid accents, glass cards, decorative blobs, excessive rounded containers, repeated icon-heading-text blocks, or meaningless numbered labels. These are valid only when the context genuinely calls for them.

## Inspect and Refine

Check the rendered image at full size and thumbnail size. Confirm that the focal point is immediate, text is legible, contrast is deliberate, spacing has rhythm, and every decorative element earns its place. Revise the composition when it merely works but does not yet feel intentional.
